# Political Systems

## Description

The **Political Systems** hub captures knowledge about the structures and frameworks by which societies are organized and governed.

This includes democracy, monarchy, authoritarianism, totalitarianism, theocracy, federalism, confederation models, and hybrid systems.  
It also covers political theory relating to how power is distributed, legitimacy is maintained, authority is exercised, and governance structures adapt over time.

Use this hub to map notes about:

- Types of governance (e.g., presidential systems, parliamentary systems, constitutional monarchies)
- Centralized vs decentralized governance structures
- Comparative political systems across different nations and eras
- Evolution of political systems historically (e.g., from feudalism to nation-states)
- Key theories about state formation, sovereignty, and legitimacy
- Influence of economic, cultural, and technological factors on political system design
- Challenges to political systems: revolutions, regime change, political reform

This hub connects to `[[core/politics]]`, and may also intersect with `[[parent/law & constitutional frameworks]]`, `[[parent/political ideologies]]`, and `[[parent/political history]]` for related structures and their evolution over time.
