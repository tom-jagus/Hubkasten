# Knowledge & Belief

## Description

The **Knowledge & Belief** hub captures knowledge related to how we come to know things, what counts as justified belief, and how we distinguish between truth, opinion, and ignorance.

This includes classical epistemology, skepticism, theories of justification, rationalism vs empiricism, the Gettier problem, belief formation, and the limits of human understanding.  
It also touches on the psychology of belief, social epistemology, and the impact of information systems (media, education, science) on what we accept as true.

Use this hub to map notes about:

- Definitions and conditions of knowledge (e.g. justified true belief)
- Sources of knowledge: reason, perception, memory, testimony
- Rationalism, empiricism, constructivism, and skepticism
- Theories of justification (coherentism, foundationalism, reliabilism)
- Epistemic virtues and vices (e.g. open-mindedness, dogmatism)
- Belief vs knowledge in religion, science, politics, and culture
- Cognitive bias, misinformation, and belief formation in society

This hub connects to `[[core/philosophy]]`, and may overlap with `[[parent/logic & reasoning]]`, `[[core/science]]`, and `[[core/culture]]` when dealing with truth claims or epistemic conflict in public discourse.
